(∼p ∧ q) ∨ (p ∧ ∼q)

Q.2. Construct the switching circuit of the following:

(ii) (p ∧ q) ∨ [∼ p ∧ (∼ q ∨ p ∨ r)]

(4 Marks)

Solution:

Step 1: Assigning variables to the switches. [1 Mark]

  • Let p: The switch S1 is closed.

  • Let r: The switch S3 is closed.
  • Let ∼ p: The switch S′1 is closed.
  • Let ∼ q: The switch S′2 is closed.
  • Let L: The lamp L is on.
ONLY FOR Explanation: Take a close look at the given logical statement. The variables involved are p, q, and r, which correspond to the primary switches S1, S2, and S3. The negations ∼ p and ∼ q dictate that we also need to include their physical complements, S′1 and S′2, in our circuit diagram.

Step 2: Analyzing the logical expression. [1 Mark]

The given logical expression is (p ∧ q) ∨ [∼ p ∧ (∼ q ∨ p ∨ r)]. The main connective is ∨ (OR), dividing the circuit into two main parallel branches:

  • Upper Branch: The first part is (p ∧ q). This signifies that switch S1 and switch S2 are connected in series.
  • Lower Branch: The second part is [∼ p ∧ (∼ q ∨ p ∨ r)]. Here, switch S′1 is in series with a parallel block.
  • Inside the Parallel Block: The sub-expression is (∼ q ∨ p ∨ r). This means three switches—S′2, S1, and S3—are connected in parallel with each other.
ONLY FOR Explanation: Always break complex expressions into smaller "chunks" or blocks. First, we identify the main split (the OR). Then we build the top wire. Then we build the bottom wire, which is a bit complex: the current must flow through S′1 first, and then it gets to choose between three separate parallel wires containing S′2, S1, and S3!
